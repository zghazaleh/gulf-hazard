Assets at risk

Values at each asset under the active scenario (A) at the selected hour. Capacities are public figures, rounded; populations are approximate (latest public statistics). Fujairah plants sit on the Gulf of Oman and are the water-supply fallback.

Wind basis

Ensemble members perturb the forecast with a direction error growing from ±10° to ±60° over seven days and a speed error from ±15% to ±50%, correlated hour to hour; beyond the forecast horizon they sample the November regime set in the assumptions table. Dispersion parameters are sampled too (deposition velocity, mixing depth, transport factor, spread rate; oil windage and current speed).

Calibration · two observed spills

GEM No.2 (October 2026). Struck about 9 October near the Strait; the UAE confirmed oil on Umm Al Quwain beaches on 23 October and at Al Hamra (Ras Al Khaimah) by 28 October. The source position was not published. Hindcast from the assumed Hormuz-approach position under October-typical winds (NW 3–4 m/s, sea-breeze modulated), the model strands on the Musandam–RAK coast within 2–4 days with the literature current (NE along the coast). Reaching Umm Al Quwain in 14 days needs a net south-westward along-coast drift of 7–10 km/day that the NE current cannot supply.

Mina Al-Ahmadi (January 1991). The Sea Island releases began 19–20 January. Oil reached the Saudi coast at Khafji in about five days and the Abu Ali headland north of Jubail in about three weeks, where it was held. Hindcast with the engine (January climatology NW 6 m/s, coastal current 0.09 m/s toward SE, 3% windage): Khafji at 2.2 days, Abu Ali at 8.2 days, i.e. 2.3–2.6 times faster than observed.

What changed as a result. Both cases say the 3% wind rule plus the literature currents move oil two to three times faster than the Gulf did. Oil arrival is therefore reported as a window from the model time to 2.5× the model time; the ensemble samples windage 1.5–3.5% instead of 2–4%; and the UAE coastal current direction (NE per the gyre literature, SW per the October drift) is sampled 50/50. The single track keeps 3% windage as the fast, planning-conservative case.

Source term and dose logic

Inventory
Bushehr-1 (VVER-1000, 3,000 MWt): about 72 t of fuel in the core and 210 t of spent fuel on site (Rosatom, Mar 2026). Equilibrium core Cs-137 of order 250–300 PBq; the spent-fuel pool holds roughly twice that again, partly decayed.
Moderate
Containment breach with core damage, 2–5% Cs-137 release: ≈10 PBq to the atmosphere (Fukushima Dai-ichi released 6–20 PBq).
Severe
Core plus spent-fuel-pool fire, 10–30% release: ≈85 PBq (Chernobyl released ≈85 PBq Cs-137).
Barakah
Four APR-1400 units (4,000 MWt each): a single-unit event scales like Bushehr; the same two classes are applied.
Deposition
Gaussian puffs released every 12 h for 72 h; σ = 2 km + 7% of distance; mixing depth 1,000 m; dry deposition velocity 0.002 m/s with along-path depletion. Wet deposition is not modelled; rain days are flagged.
Dose rate
Early-phase ambient dose rate ≈ 15 µSv/h per MBq/m² of Cs-137-equivalent deposition (Fukushima, late March 2011), falling to ≈2 µSv/h per MBq/m² after a year.
OILs
IAEA GSR Part 7 / EPR defaults: OIL1 1,000 µSv/h (evacuate or shelter), OIL2 100 µSv/h (relocate, first 10 days), OIL3 1 µSv/h (restrict local food, milk, water). In deposition terms: 67 MBq/m², 6.7 MBq/m², 67 kBq/m². "Detectable" is 1 kBq/m².
Smoke
Soot yield 2% of fuel burned; burn rates per facility are illustrative; ground-level PM2.5 from a well-mixed 1,000 m layer. Bands: 250 µg/m³ hazardous, 55 unhealthy, 15 elevated (WHO 24-h guideline), 2 trace.

Limits

  • Gaussian-puff and kinematic drift, not HYSPLIT or GNOME. Deposition is dry only; rain under the cloud (forecast 6–7 Nov at Bushehr) produces hot spots the bands do not show.
  • Dose rates come from a single Cs-137-equivalent factor; inhalation and iodine pathways are not computed. OIL comparison is indicative.
  • Smoke concentrations assume a well-mixed layer; a lofted plume gives lower ground values near the fire and higher values where it touches down.
  • Tanker positions are scenario choices, not AIS. Placing a custom source by clicking the map is the substitute until a Windward or MarineTraffic feed is connected.
  • Wind beyond 4 Nov is speed-only forecast with assumed direction; beyond 9 Nov it is climatology. The ensemble shows this as widening spread, not as knowledge.
  • Arabic covers the console and readouts; evidence sections are English only.
  • Operational guidance sits with FANR, NCEMA, MoCCAE and DEWA.
